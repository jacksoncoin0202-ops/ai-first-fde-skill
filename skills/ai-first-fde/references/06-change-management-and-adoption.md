# Change Management and Adoption

## Adoption is part of deployment

A technically correct system that nobody uses is not deployed.

## Adoption assets

- Manager announcement script
- Employee FAQ
- Training deck
- Office hour schedule
- Champion program
- No-blame pilot policy
- Feedback channel
- Usage dashboard

## Relationship handling by group

| Group | Likely concern | Do this | Avoid this |
|---|---|---|---|
| Executive sponsor | ROI, speed, visible success | Use one measurable pilot and weekly blocker review | abstract strategy deck |
| Legal / compliance / security | data exposure, audit, policy breach | involve them before PoC; show PII handling, logging, and approval gates | asking for approval after building |
| IT / data owner | integration load, support burden | define narrow data path, owner, monitoring, rollback | dumping undefined tool support onto IT |
| Daily users | extra work, inconsistent answers | train on real cases; show when AI is good enough and when to escalate | claiming perfect accuracy |
| Frontline workers | replacement, blame | position AI as removing repetitive work; name redeployment / higher-value work | cost-cutting language |
| Senior staff | loss of status or know-how | make them reviewers and knowledge owners | making them look obsolete |
| Middle managers | loss of control, unclear responsibility | give manager script, KPI, and escalation path | bypassing them publicly |

## Messaging patterns

Good:

- "AI reduces rework and helps teams find information faster."
- "Senior staff will review and improve the AI knowledge base."
- "Pilot errors are learning data, not individual failure."
- "This pilot removes repetitive work first. Existing experts keep judgment and review ownership."
- "We will measure one workflow KPI before expanding."

Avoid:

- "AI will replace manual workers."
- "Everyone must use this immediately."
- "The old way is stupid."
- "The model is smart enough; process does not matter."
- "Security can review this later."

## Weekly adoption loop

1. Check usage by team.
2. Ask three users what slowed them down.
3. Review one successful case and one failed case.
4. Classify the issue: awareness, skill, trust, incentive, status, manager signal, workflow mismatch, or policy block.
5. Assign one fix.
6. Confirm whether to continue, narrow, or pause.
